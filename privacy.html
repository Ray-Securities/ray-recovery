Privacy policy

Effective 7 November 2026 · Ray Securities

This policy covers version 2.0 and later of the Android app Ray File Recovery (package com.raysecurities.recovery, listed on Google Play as “Ray Video Recovery”), published by Ray Securities in Bangladesh. Here “Ray” means the app, and “we” and “us” mean Ray Securities.

The short version

Ray works on your phone. It has no account, no ads, no analytics and no servers, so it sends nothing off your phone by itself, and we never receive your photos, videos, audio or contacts. Something leaves your phone only when you choose: when you share a file or open it in another app, open a photo's location in a maps app, email us, or follow a link.

1. Everything happens on your phone

Ray reads Android's media library and, if you allow it, your contacts, and shows the results on your screen. Scans such as finding large files or duplicate photos run on the phone. Ray changes your files only when you tell it to: it moves items to Android's trash, restores them or deletes them, and it asks you to confirm before anything is trashed or deleted. On Android 11 and newer, Android asks you to confirm as well.

We run no servers that receive your data, and the app has no sign-in. Ray makes no internet requests of its own and contains no advertising or analytics code. We collect no personal data, no device identifiers and no usage statistics, and nothing is sold or shared with advertisers or data brokers.

2. Permissions and why Ray needs them

Photos, videos and audio

READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO and READ_MEDIA_AUDIO (Android 13 and newer); READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED (Android 14 and newer); READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE and WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE (Android 12 and older only)

To list and preview your media, show what is in Android's trash and restore it, save copies, and find large files, duplicates and old screenshots. On Android 14 and newer you can allow selected photos and videos only; Ray then works with just those, and items in the trash stay hidden. On Android 10 and older, Android's storage permission covers all files in shared storage; Ray uses it only to read photos, videos and audio and to save its own copies and backups.

Photo location

ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION

Lets Ray keep the location stored in a photo when you save a copy, and show it under Details. Ray reads it on the phone and never uploads it. If you tap Location under Details, Ray opens your maps app at that spot, and that app's privacy policy applies.

Contacts

READ_CONTACTS

Used when you open Contacts backup: Ray counts your contacts, makes backups and compares a backup with the contacts on your phone. A backup holds each contact's name and nickname, company, department and job title, phone numbers, email and postal addresses, websites, birthday and notes. Contact photos and groups are left out.

Ray cannot add, change or delete contacts. Restoring hands the backup file to your Contacts app, which asks you before adding anything.

Other apps on your phone

Package visibility for Google Photos, Files by Google, Samsung Gallery and Xiaomi Gallery

Lets Ray check whether these apps are installed and open them from “Other places to check”, because they keep their own trash. Opening one sends it nothing. Android also lets Ray see which apps can receive a shared file, open a link or a document, or edit a contact, so they can appear when you share or open something. Ray keeps no list of your apps and sends none anywhere.

Granted without asking

INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, VIBRATE

Android grants these to any app that declares them. Ray declares them because the libraries it is built on, such as its image and video components, include them; Ray itself makes no internet requests. Vibration is used for short haptic feedback.

Ray does not ask for your current (GPS) location, camera, microphone, phone, SMS, notifications or “All files access”, and it does not run in the background.

3. What Ray keeps on your phone

  • Your theme choice, whether you have seen the welcome tour, and a few technical settings, such as which permissions Ray has already asked for.
  • A history of what you did in Ray, such as saving copies or restoring items: the action, how many items, and for contacts backups the file name. Ray keeps the latest 200 entries.
  • The newest five contacts backups, so Ray can tell you which contacts are missing.
  • A cache: small thumbnails of items in Android's trash, resized copies of pictures shown on screen, and the contact files used when you restore from a backup. Android can clear the cache at any time. Ray deletes a contact file the next time it starts once the file is an hour old, and it removes the thumbnails of anything you delete for good.

All of this stays in Ray's private storage on the phone. It is excluded from Android and Google backups and from device-to-device transfer. Settings › Delete Ray's data removes all of it except the technical settings, which hold nothing about you, and Android deletes everything when you uninstall the app.

4. Files Ray writes to your storage

When you tap Save copy, Ray copies the file into a “Ray Recovery” album in Pictures (in Music for audio). The original is never moved or changed. When you back up contacts, Ray also saves the backup file to Download/Ray Recovery when it can; if it can't, it tells you and keeps the backup inside the app.

These files belong to you. You can move or delete them with any file manager, and they stay on the phone after Ray is uninstalled. Like any shared file, other apps with access to your files, including backup and phone-transfer apps you use, can read or copy them. A contacts backup holds the details listed under Contacts above, so keep it somewhere safe.

Version 1 of the app used folders named “Recovered Photos”, “Recovered Videos”, “Recovered Audios”, “Recovered Documents”, “Recovered WhatsApp” and “Recovered Status”, and it moved files into them instead of copying. Files in those folders may be your only copy, so check before you delete them.

5. When something leaves your phone

Ray sends nothing off your phone by itself. Something leaves it only when you choose to:

  • Share or Open with: Android hands the file to the app you pick.
  • Location under Details: your maps app opens at that spot.
  • Send feedback: your email app opens a message to us with the app version, your phone model and Android version filled in. You can change or remove them before sending.
  • Links: Help, Privacy and Terms open this website, which is hosted on GitHub Pages. Rate opens Google Play, and “Other places to check” may open the Google Photos website. They open in your browser or the Play Store app.

What those apps and websites do with the data is covered by their own privacy policies.

6. How your data is protected

Ray's own data stays in its private app storage, which other apps can't read, and it is never sent anywhere, so there is no copy on a server to leak. Saved copies and contacts backups are ordinary shared files: keep your phone locked and delete backups you no longer need.

7. Crash reports

Ray does not send crash reports. If you let your phone share usage and diagnostics data with Google, Android may send crash information to Google, which shows it to developers as anonymous reports and statistics in Google Play Console. If Ray ever adds crash reports of its own, this section will say what is sent (never your files or contacts), and the date at the top will change before an update turns it on.

8. If you email us

We receive your email address, your message, anything you attach and, if you used Send feedback, the app version, phone model and Android version. We use them only to answer you, and we delete them if you ask.

9. Children

Ray is a general-audience utility. It is not directed at children under 13, and it collects no data from anyone.

10. Changes and contact

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top. Questions or requests: hello@raysecurities.com. See also how to delete your data.
